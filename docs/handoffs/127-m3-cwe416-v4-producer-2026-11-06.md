## 2. The candidate's `unsat` is the empty-disjunction case — say it plainly

The repair replaced `call free@plt` with five `nop`s. On the candidate's single
path nothing is ever freed, so the disjunction over freed pointers is empty and
the property question is unsatisfiable. That **is** an answer to the question,
and `free_calls: 0` is the evidence for it.

It is not a solved constraint, and the file says so twice — in the case `notes`
and in `not_claimed` ("a deep proof on the repaired side: its unsat is the
empty-disjunction case"). A reader who sees `unsat: 1` and assumes z3 did work
would be over-reading; this record exists partly to stop that.

## 3. Why this is `model` and still not binary-level

The analysis reads the ELF, resolves `target_entry` from the contract
(`0x4012aa`), and explores the target function's paths. It does not prove
anything about the rest of the program, about a different allocator, or about
the C source. `scopes.py` maps `symbolic → model` regardless of outcome, and
`not_claimed` repeats it: no binary-level reading.

The four hooks are enumerated with justifications in the evidence. None of them
assumes the repair is safe: `free` records, `printLine` observes, `memset` is a
no-op because the property is about pointers, `malloc` exists to make the one
remaining nondeterminism explicit, and `exit` is hooked so the malloc-null path
does not become an unaccounted unconstrained state.
